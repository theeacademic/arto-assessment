AI USE DISCLOSURE
Kenya population by county, 2021-2025 — AHADI technical assessment
Kelly Nyawira, 16 September 2026


WHICH TOOL
-----------
Claude (Anthropic), used through Claude Code in my terminal. No other AI tool was
used. Claude wrote most of the Python in this repository, and it drafted this README
and this log. The analysis decisions were mine, and I have recorded below what I was
asked and what I chose, because those choices are the part that matters.


HOW I WORKED WITH IT
--------------------
I did not hand it the brief and ask for a solution. I set the ground rules up front:
work in four stages and stop after each one for my go-ahead; before writing code for
a stage, give me two or three options with tradeoffs and let me pick; never decide for
me; flag ambiguities in the brief instead of resolving them; never silently repair
data; and write plain-English notes after each stage so I can explain the code.

That structure is why this log is mostly a list of decisions rather than a list of
features. Claude did the typing and the measuring. I did the choosing.


MY PROMPTS, BY SECTION
----------------------

Setting up. My opening prompt gave the rules above, pointed at the brief, and set the
scope: Kenya only, WorldPop 1 km age and sex rasters 2021-2025, aggregated to GADM
boundaries, plus a Streamlit dashboard. I asked it to read the spec and report back in
ten bullets what was required, flagging anything ambiguous rather than resolving it. I
also told it explicitly not to write the README, the interpretation section, the
public-health commentary or the policy implications.

Stage 1, scaffold. "go ahead with stage 1". I asked for the repo structure, a
requirements.txt, a .gitignore and empty module stubs with docstrings only. No logic.

Stage 2, ingest and validate. "go ahead with stage 2". Programmatic download with
on-disk caching, a filename parser for year, sex and age band, a check that every
expected age-sex-year combination is present, CRS checks on rasters and boundaries,
negative and zero value checks, everything written to a validation log, nothing
silently repaired.

Stage 3, aggregate. "go ahead with stage 3". Zonal sums per polygon per age-sex-year,
the derived indicators exactly as named in the brief, written to the exact CSV schema
specified, then the three static plots.

Stage 4, dashboard. "go". Streamlit, reading only the processed CSV plus boundaries.
Filters for year, sex, indicator and optional county. Two visualisations only, a
choropleth and an age pyramid. Plain layout.

Deployment. "shut down the local server and tell me how to deploy this to streamlit",
then "un-ignore the gadm file and keep one requirements.txt or do whatever is
recommended to enable the deployment".

Documentation. "write a human made readme.md and ai usage log in docs also in human
based form/language include prompts for different sections and what ai handled make it
minimal". This reversed my earlier instruction that the README was mine to write, so
Claude drafted it and I edited it. I am noting that here rather than leaving it
implied.


WHAT I DECIDED, STAGE BY STAGE
------------------------------

Reading the brief. Claude came back with ten bullets and eight ambiguities. Two were
substantive and I left both open rather than let it pick. The brief says
"unconstrained" but gives a path ending in "constrained"; and it calls GADM level 2
"counties" when level 2 is actually 300 constituencies and the 47 counties live in a
different field.

Stage 1. It offered me the brief's example tree verbatim, a leaner tree, or the lean
tree plus a config module. I took the last one. I did not want empty test files I had
no intention of filling, and I wanted the URL pattern and age bands stated once so the
decisions I had not yet made would sit in a single reviewable place. It left five
constants set to None with the open question written next to each. On dependencies I
chose floor pins over exact pins, because a resolver conflict during a timed exercise
is a worse risk than a loose minimum.

Stage 2. Before offering options it timed an actual download and told me a sequential
fetch of all 300 files would take about 87 minutes, roughly half my budget. That
changed what I chose. I had it verify all 300 files exist with cheap HEAD requests but
download only the 200 male and female ones, so I could honestly say the completeness
check covered the full set without paying to transfer a third of it. For URL discovery
I chose the REST API with a fallback to a constructed pattern, so a third-party outage
could not stop a timed run. For the county question I chose GADM's NAME_1 field and
47 counties, treating the brief's "Level 2 (Counties)" label as an error in the brief.
I also chose to defer the pixel-value checks to the aggregation stage, since that
stage has to read every raster anyway.

Stage 3. Claude benchmarked two approaches rather than guessing: rasterstats at about
two and a half minutes for 200 rasters, against rasterizing the counties once and
using numpy at about three seconds. I took the fast one but asked for one raster to be
re-summed with rasterstats as an independent check, with the agreement written into
the validation log. They agree to within 0.03%, which is pixels on county boundaries.
On nodata I insisted the -99999 marker and a genuinely negative population be treated
as different problems: mask the first, halt on the second. There were no genuine
negatives. I confirmed the age band groupings and chose to write a second long-format
CSV, because the required schema carries no sex and no age band and the dashboard
needed both.

Stage 4. The sex toggle was the interesting decision. Three of the seven required
indicators are counts that can be split by sex; the other four are ratios that cannot,
since a sex ratio is males divided by females and there is no such thing as one for
males alone. I chose to disable the toggle with a short explanation rather than return
a number that looks meaningful but is not. I also chose a plain projection over a
street basemap, and both a dropdown and click-on-map for selecting counties.

Deployment. Claude found a problem I had missed: the dashboard reads the GADM file,
but that file sat inside a gitignored folder, so a deploy would have started and then
crashed. I chose to un-ignore that single file and keep one requirements.txt, at the
cost of a slower cloud build.


HOW I CHECKED THE CODE
----------------------
I read every file before running it, and I checked its claims against things I could
verify independently rather than taking its word.

I confirmed the WorldPop REST API really does list 60 files per year for Kenya under
the path the brief hints at, and that the GADM file really does contain 300
constituency features with 47 distinct county names.

On the outputs, I checked the numbers against reality. National totals run from 52.6
million in 2021 to 56.9 million in 2025, which is the right order against the 47.6
million counted in the 2019 census. Nairobi's computed area came out at about 700
square kilometres against an actual 696. The arithmetic identity holds: under-fives
plus working age plus over-65s plus ages 5 to 14 equals the total, to within two
people across all 235 county-years. The sex splits go the way demography says they
should, with more boys than girls under five and substantially more women than men
over 65. I opened all three figures and confirmed the raster map is recognisably
Kenya, with the dense western highlands, the Nairobi hotspot and the empty arid north.

For the dashboard I ran it through Streamlit's own headless test harness rather than
just clicking around: it runs with no exception, both charts render, the sex toggle
disables when a ratio is selected, and changing the year or county updates both
panels. For the deployment fix I had it rebuild exactly what a fresh clone would
contain, with no rasters and no virtual environment, and run the app there. It worked,
which is the check that actually proves the deploy will hold.

Two defects surfaced that way and were fixed: a deprecated Streamlit argument that
warned on every render, and the gitignored boundary file described above.


A LATE ADDITION
---------------
After the four stages I asked for a small test script to validate the reproducibility
claims in the README, and told Claude to keep it simple. I chose two constraints: no
pytest, so requirements.txt does not grow, and it must validate only the committed
files so it runs on a fresh clone without downloading 300 MB of rasters first.

It produced tests/test_reproducibility.py, which checks the CSV schema and row counts,
the county names against the boundary file, that the age groups sum back to the total,
that every ratio recomputes from its own components, that the two CSVs agree, and that
the national totals quoted in the README are what the data holds. Twenty-seven checks
pass with the raster cache present, twenty-four with two skipped without it.

I asked it to prove the test could actually fail, since a test that cannot fail proves
nothing. It corrupted one county's working-age figure in a throwaway copy and confirmed
four checks failed and the script exited non-zero.


WHAT IS MINE
------------
The choice of approach at every stage, the resolution of the brief's contradictions,
the insistence that nodata and negative values are different problems, the decision to
disable rather than fake the sex toggle, and the interpretation of what the numbers
mean. The observation that the brief's dependency ratio is not the conventional one,
and therefore produces values near 20 to 40 rather than the published figure near 70,
is mine and matters for anyone reading the output.
